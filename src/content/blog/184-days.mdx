Two days before USS Jouett (DLG-29) left for Vietnam, Fire Control Technician Mike Ducy sat down to write his parents in Decatur, Illinois.

> *"Things have been pretty busy on the ship in the past week. We have had a lot of inspections and they have been loading stores about every day."*
>
> — [Mike Ducy to Home, January 14, 1968](/day/1968-01-14)

He mentioned serving mass for the chaplain. Driving a buddy to pick up the mail. Seeing *Valley of the Dolls*. And almost as an aside: *"I was suppose to meet Bill here Friday evening, but he never made it for some reason. Maybe he is on his way over [to Vietnam] now."*

Bill was his younger brother, a Marine, already bound for Da Nang. They wouldn't see each other again until [April 16th, in a combat zone](/day/1968-04-16), aboard the USS Jouett in Da Nang Harbor.

## "Cast Off All Lines"

The cruise book captures the morning of [January 16, 1968](/day/1968-01-16) and the somber mood:

> *On the morning of January 16, 1968, JOUETT cast off all lines, put both engines astern, and backed away from the pier, leaving wives and children, sweethearts and friends behind. The long months of training and preparation had borne fruit. For the first time, DLG-29 proceeded to conflict, ready to serve.*

Mike's logbook is more to the matter:

> *1000 — Underway for Pearl Harbor from Nav Station San Diego, Calif. Condition III Set. Steaming with USS Preble (DLG-15). ComDesRon One Embarked.*

No sentiment. No mention of the families on the pier. Just the time, the destination, and the formation. That's the logbook — every day for 184 days, the same disciplined shorthand. The emotion lives in the letters and tape recordings he sent home.

Jouett steamed west with Destroyer Squadron One: [five days to Pearl Harbor](/day/1968-01-17), [liberty in Waikiki](/day/1968-01-22), then onward through [Guam](/day/1968-02-05) and [Subic Bay](/day/1968-02-08). A routine of watches, exercises, and approaches with the escort ships, USS Preble and USS Hull.

## "Have M-1 Rifles at Hand"

[February 15th, Day 30](/day/1968-02-15). One line brings the reality of the situation to bear:

> *Entered Vietnamese Waters Early in the Morning. 0810 — Anchor in Da Nang Harbor. Have M-1 Rifles at Hand in Topside Working Spaces.*

The day before had been Valentine's Day. A helicopter [nearly hit the water when a cargo door opened](/day/1968-02-14), causing damage but landing safely. They fired 81mm mortars and observed tropical working hours for the first time. By February 16th, Mike was [standing sentry with an Officer at 0600](/day/1968-02-16), and Jouett was underway for her first line duty at South SAR station. The Search and Rescue station in the Gulf of Tonkin that would define Jouett's deployment.

## 103 Days on Station

What followed was a deployment of extraordinary operational tempo. Jouett served as lynchpin of the Navy's search and rescue units in the Tonkin Gulf, sailing between the aircraft carriers operating as part of Operation Rolling Thunder and the North Vietnamese coast. The ship completed 932 helicopter takeoffs and landings, made 25 night underway replenishments, and spent 103 days in the combat zone without a serious accident among her crew of 400.

Mike's logbook records every day in the same factual amnner. [Eight aviators rescued](/day/1968-04-28). [Terrier missiles fired at MiGs](/day/1968-06-17). [A MiG-21 downed](/day/1968-06-26). But also: [movies on the fantail](/day/1968-05-05). [Pay days](/day/1968-03-01). And more importantly, the weather, which has been helpful in recreating key pieces of Mike's journey in relation to artifacts in the archive. 

On [April 16th](/day/1968-04-16), Jouett anchored in Da Nang Harbor. At 1230, Mike's brother Bill came aboard. They got four hours together, reconnecting and recording a tape home. After Bill left at 1630, Mike stood sentry with a .50 caliber machine gun, watching small craft in the harbor.

Twelve days later, on [April 28th](/day/1968-04-28), Jouett made its first major rescue. An F-4B Phantom from VF-21 "Freelancers" off USS Ranger was hit by 85mm anti-aircraft fire near Vinh. Both pilots — LCDR Diego "Duke" Hernandez and LTJG David Lortschey — were rescued in 29 minutes.

In between the operations, Mike sent tape recordings home on cassettes. His family in Decatur would gather around the player and listen, then record a tape back. These tapes survived, 23 in total [now digitized and transcribed](/archive#audio), a most intimate record of what deployment sounded like and how the family tried to keep him connected to events at home.

## The Long Way Home

[July 1, Day 167](/day/1968-07-01). Underway for Subic Bay. The combat deployment was over and the USS Jouett completed it's third line duty on South SAR Station. They ended the deployment with 5 successful SAR missions, & 8 rescued aviators.

They [offloaded ammunition at Subic](/day/1968-07-02), steamed through the [San Bernardino Straits past an active volcano](/day/1968-07-03), and set course east. On [July 9th](/day/1968-07-09), Davy Jones appeared to summon the pollywogs. The next morning — [July 10th, Day 176](/day/1968-07-10) — Jouett crossed the equator at the 180th meridian, and King Neptune's court held a Shellback initiation ceremony. The date line crossing meant the ship lived two Wednesdays.

A [quick stop at Pearl Harbor on July 13th](/day/1968-07-13) for fuel, stores, and mail — five letters and a package of books — then the final leg to San Diego.

The last entries wind down:

> *[Day 182](/day/1968-07-16). Underway for San Diego. Warm, rainy, cooler, high seas. 0800 — Pay Day, drew $201.00.*
>
> *[Day 183](/day/1968-07-17). 0830 — Conduct hi-line transfer of personnel from USS Hull. 1530 — Safe driving lecture by California State Trooper.*

A safe driving lecture. After six months in a combat zone, someone had the audacity to lecture them about California traffic.

## Homecoming and the "Holiday Routine" PoD

[July 18, 1968. Day 184.](/day/1968-07-18)

> *1011 — Arrive and Moor Pier #4, U.S. Naval Station, San Diego. Holiday Routine.*

The San Diego Union ran the story the next morning:

> *Bands played, banners fluttered, and wives and children waved yesterday as three vessels of San Diego-based Destroyer Division One returned home from six months' Vietnam action.*
>
> *The Jouett wore eight parachutes painted on the superstructure below her bridge, signifying eight aviators were rescued during the deployment.*

The cruise book shows the scene: families on the pier with hand-lettered signs — *"Welcome Home Dad and Dave,"* *"HI!!!! RICHARD RD3"* — women looking up at the ship through binoculars, a sailor kissing his wife while his daughter held a sign that read *"Daddy."*

The Plan of the Day for July 18th had specified uniforms: Tropical White for entering port. It listed the tide tables and the watch bill. It did not mention that the men hadn't been home in half a year.

Mike Ducy was 20 years old. His logbook — 184 days of terse, factual entries written in a young sailor's hand — is now [browsable day by day](/day/1968-01-16) alongside his letters, audio tapes, photographs, and official records. His brother Bill's Marine service in Da Nang runs in parallel. The family's voice, on cassette tapes recorded from Decatur, fills in what the logbook leaves out.
