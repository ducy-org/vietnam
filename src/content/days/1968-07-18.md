## Logbook

1011 — ARRIVE AND MOOR PIER #4, U.S. NAVAL STATION, SAN DIEGO. Holiday routine. END OF DEPLOYMENT.

## Sources

> From the Plan of the Day, 18 July 1968: At 0830, a press helicopter took aerial photographs as the crew — in Tropical Whites — formed the number "29" on the flight deck, visible from the Cabrillo Monument on Point Loma. A large sign on the port side read "Happiness is Homecoming." Balloons streamed from the forecastle and fantail. After mooring at Pier 4, refreshments were served on the helo deck and mess decks; dependents and guests were invited aboard for tours. Signed by LCDR J. A. Walker, Jr., Executive Officer. [Plan of the Day]

> From the San Diego Union, 19 July 1968 — "Three Warships Return From Vietnam Duty": "The Jouett, with Capt. A. T. Emerson Jr., squadron commander, aboard, wore eight parachutes painted on the superstructure below her bridge, signifying eight aviators were rescued during the deployment." Emerson, of Coronado, had served as commander of the Navy's search and rescue units in the Tonkin Gulf. Capt. Robert S. Hayes reported 932 helicopter takeoffs and landings, 25 night replenishments, and 103 days in the combat zone without a serious accident among his crew of 400. USS Preble (DLG-15) — the ship from which Lassen's Medal of Honor rescue launched — and USS Hull (DD-945) returned alongside. Hull had fired 25,000 five-inch shells, believed to be a record for a single deployment. [San Diego Union]

> From the Jul 5 Familygram: "I can assure you that your JOUETTmen are returning home in good health and with a great pride in their achievements." [Familygram]
